# Texas Medicaid Specialty Provider Network Standards

## Overview

Texas Medicaid managed care covers approximately 97.1% of the state's Medicaid enrollment (FY 2023). The program is administered by the Texas Health and Human Services Commission (HHSC) and delivered through 16 Managed Care Organizations (MCOs) and 3 Dental Maintenance Organizations (DMOs) across distinct geographic service areas (SAs).

**Managed Care Programs:**
- **STAR** — Children and pregnant women
- **STAR Health** — Statewide program for adults age 21+
- **STAR+PLUS** — Aged, blind, and disabled (includes LTSS)
- **STAR Kids** — Children with special health care needs (CSHCN)
- **CHIP** — Children's Health Insurance Program
- **CMDS** — Children's Medicaid Dental Services (administered by DMOs)
- **S+P MMP** — STAR+PLUS Managed Medical Partnership

**Network Oversight:** HHSC monitors MCO compliance quarterly through geomap analysis, comparing provider locations against member enrollment on a county-by-county basis. MCOs failing to meet standards face corrective action plans (CAPs) and liquidated damages (LD).

## Specialty Categories and Travel Time Standards

Texas uses a three-tier county designation system based on population density:
- **Metro** — Urban counties
- **Micro** — Suburban/small urban counties
- **Rural** — Low-density counties

Standards are expressed as maximum travel time (minutes) to reach a required number of providers. The default performance threshold is 90% of members within the standard.

## Key Differences from Other States

1. **Three-tier county system** — Unlike most states that use urban/rural binary, Texas uses Metro/Micro/Rural tiers, creating more granular standards.
2. **DMO-administered dental** — Dental services for children are carved out to separate Dental Maintenance Organizations (DMOs), not managed by MCOs (except STAR Health where dental is carved-in).
3. **LTSS "choice of 2 per county"** — In-home services use a county-based standard rather than travel time, reflecting the nature of in-home delivery.
4. **MRSA pharmacy carve-out** — Metropolitan Rural Service Areas (MRSAs) have separate, lower pharmacy thresholds.
5. **16 MCOs + 3 DMOs** — Highly fragmented network across many service areas.
6. **SB 760 framework** — Texas network adequacy standards were specifically developed under state legislation (S.B. 760, 84th Legislature, 2015), which mandated HHSC to develop specific provider network access standards.
7. **Telemedicine exclusion** — Providers who only offer telehealth/telemedicine services cannot be counted toward MCO access standards.
