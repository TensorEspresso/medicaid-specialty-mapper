## Georgia Core Specialists

### Primary Care
- PCPs (Family Practice, General Practice, Internal Medicine, NP, PA, FQHC)
- Pediatricians

### Medical Specialties
- Adult Medical (Allergy, Cardiology, Dermatology, Endocrinology, ENT, Gastroenterology, General Surgery, Hematology/Oncology, Infectious Disease, Nephrology, Neurology, Ophthalmology, Optometry, Orthopedics, Rheumatology, Urology, Vascular Surgery)
- Pediatric Medical (Matching adult list)

### Other Core Services
- OB/GYN
- Behavioral Health (Psychiatrists, Psychologists, ASD Treatment, Narcotic Treatment Programs, Licensed Counselors/Social Workers)
- Dental (General and Subspecialty)
- Ancillary (Dialysis, DME, Hospitals, Lab, Radiology, Therapy, Vision)

## Key Notes

- **Distance-Based Only**: Georgia uses miles-based standards rather than the time-or-distance approach used by most states.
- **Pediatric Granularity**: Georgia tracks 17 pediatric specialties separately from adult specialties, doubling the specialist count.
- **Driving Distance**: Compliance is measured using driving distance, not radial (as-the-crow-flies) distance.
- **Regional Structure**: The state is divided into 6 Managed Care Regions (Atlanta, Central, East, North, Southeast, Southwest).

## Data Gaps

1. **Official Crosswalk**: No official Georgia Medicaid-to-NUCC taxonomy crosswalk is published.
2. **Source Accessibility**: The authoritative Georgia Families Contract is not publicly available as a standalone PDF and must be obtained via FOIA or CMO channels.
3. **Provider Ratios**: Georgia specifies minimum provider counts within distance rather than enrollee-to-provider ratios.
