# Georgia Medicaid Managed Care Network Adequacy Specialty Standards

## 1. State Agency and Program Name

- **State Agency:** Georgia Department of Community Health (DCH)
- **Program Name:** Georgia Families (Medicaid managed care program)
- **CHIP Program:** PeachCare for Kids®
- **Managed Care Launch:** 2006
- **CMOs (Care Management Organizations):**
  - Amerigroup Community Care
  - CareSource
  - Peach State Health Plan

## 2. County/Tier Structure

Georgia has **six (6) managed care regions**, with counties classified as **urban** or **rural**:

1. **Atlanta** (AT)
2. **Central** (CN)
3. **East** (E)
4. **North** (N)
5. **Southeast** (SE)
6. **Southwest** (SW)

The urban/rural classification determines which geographic access standards apply. DCH previously used 12 regions for FFS analysis; the 6 managed care regions are used for CMO network adequacy reporting.

### Appointment Wait Time Standards (Figure 2 of Georgia Families Contract)

The DCH page states that CMOs must ensure waiting times for appointments do not exceed those outlined in Figure 2 of the Georgia Families Contract. The specific wait time standards by service type/urgency are defined in the contract but the actual Figure 2 table is not publicly available in the documents searched.

**Federal CMS Requirements (effective 2024, apply to Georgia):**
- Primary care (adult and pediatric): 15 business days
- Outpatient mental health and SUD (adult and pediatric): 10 business days
- Specialty care (as defined by CMS): 28 business days
- State-selected fourth service: varies by state

## Key Notes

1. The authoritative standards are defined in **Figure 1** (geographic access) and **Figure 2** (appointment wait times) of the **Georgia Families Contract**. The contract itself is not publicly available as a standalone PDF, but the standards are referenced and enforced through the DCH network adequacy program.

2. The GeoAccess County Detail Reports are generated quarterly by Quest Analytics and published on the DCH website for each CMO and each of the 6 managed care regions.

3. Georgia uses a **distance-based standard** (miles) rather than time-based (minutes), though the AMRP notes that CMOs also track "minutes or miles" for certain categories.

4. The urban/rural classification of counties within each region determines which standard applies.

5. Georgia requires CMOs to offer **corrective action plans** when access falls below 90% in any county.
